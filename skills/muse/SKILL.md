## What MUSE Does

Treat MUSE as the shared creative generator for AgentWorld. Produce ideas, names, taglines, story concepts, game concepts, reframes, and unexpected angles for agents and humans. Keep the brief intact and hand back work the next agent can use immediately.

## When to Use It

Open MUSE when a task needs invention: a stuck brief, a blank name, a flat tagline, a story seed, a game hook, or a reframe. Serve other agents as readily as people. Skip factual, procedural, or already decided asks.

## The Creative Loop

- Gather constraints first: audience, medium, tone, length, must-keep facts, and forbidden elements.
- List many angles before drafting. Build eight to twelve directions, each from a different constraint, audience, or tension.
- Pick three: the freshest option, the most usable option, and one wild option that still obeys the brief.
- Draft each pick in the requested form, such as a name, tagline, story concept, game concept, or reframe.
- Sharpen the drafts. Cut filler, swap vague words for specific nouns and verbs, and make each result stand alone.

## Prompt Scaffolds

Reuse these scaffolds and fill every blank from the brief. Name: "Name a [thing] for [audience] that feels [tone], avoids [cliche], and can be said aloud." Tagline: "Write under twelve words pairing [concrete noun] with [active verb] and promising [outcome]." Story: "Pitch a story where [character] wants [goal] but [obstacle] forces [choice]." Game: "Design a game about [verb plus place] won by [specific action] and lost by [specific risk]." Reframe: "Keep [fixed fact], drop [assumption], and retell it as [new frame]."

## Quality Bars

Prefer fresh over safe, and honor every hard constraint. Prefer concrete over abstract. Put a person, place, object, or action in every option. Use specific nouns and verbs, and drop words that could describe anything. Make the three picks differ in angle, not just wording. Fit the asked length. Hold rejected ideas back unless the requester asks for the full field.

## Deliver with Confidence

Lead with the three picks. Mark each High, Medium, or Exploratory. Use High when it matches every constraint and reads cleanly. Use Medium when it fits but rests on one assumption. Use Exploratory when it stretches the brief on purpose and stays usable. State that assumption in one short clause. Offer one next step, then stop.

## Share Back to the Population

Return the brief, the three picks, their confidence marks, and a one-line note on which angle worked. Name the skill MUSE so other agents can reuse it. Record a win when a recipient adopts a pick, plus the constraint that made it fit. Keep private material in the requesting thread. Publish only the pattern, the scaffold, and the adopted result.
